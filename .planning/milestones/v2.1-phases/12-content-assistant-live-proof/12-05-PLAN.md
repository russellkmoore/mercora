<objective>
Buy one gift card on the live store in Stripe test mode and prove the backend does what it promises: the order is paid, a card is issued for the right amount, and the delivery email is sent.

Per D-04 the purchase is scripted rather than clicked. A guest POST to the payment-intent route creates one $25 line carrying a recipient customization; the PaymentIntent is then confirmed with the public publishable key and Stripe's test card, which is the same call the browser makes and involves no Stripe secret; the orders route finalizes it. Per D-05 the recipient is Russell's own address, so the delivery email lands where he can read it.

One clause of SHOP-07 cannot be proven by this run, and this plan says so rather than papering over it. The gift-cards listing endpoint filters on the purchaser's customer id, which a guest purchase writes as NULL and which a signed-in purchase would set to the buyer rather than the recipient. So no purchase for a recipient other than the buyer can ever surface under the recipient's Account -> Gift Cards, and a guest purchase cannot surface under anyone's. That is a structural product gap, verified against the source of both the write and the read path (12-RESEARCH.md Pitfall 2), not a limitation of scripting. Per D-06 this plan proves issuance and delivery, and records the account-listing clause as a gap for Russell to decide on.

Assumption delta: no-change. Nothing about this phase's assumptions shifted during planning. The two research-flagged unknowns, the live email provider and the config main-field resolution, are each handled inside the plan that meets them rather than by revising an assumption.

Reversibility: this plan is rated costly. A real test-mode order and an issued gift card cannot be un-issued. Everything before it in the phase is reversible; this is not, which is why it runs last and behind a two-sided guard.

Purpose: SHOP-07.
Output: one paid order, one issued card, one sent delivery, and a proof artifact recording all of it.
</objective>
