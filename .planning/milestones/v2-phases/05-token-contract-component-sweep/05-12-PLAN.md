<objective>
Sweep the six transactional email builders to the token source, then close the phase with the
whole-tree scan and the complete coverage record.

D-08 puts transactional email inside the sweep rather than outside it, and its "any other
inline-styled HTML email" clause is what pulls in the three builders CONTEXT.md does not name
by hand — including the largest one.

Purpose: `lib/utils/email.ts` alone holds 85 of the roughly 127 hex occurrences outside the
Tailwind config — more than any other file in the repository and far more than the three email
files CONTEXT.md names. It is also the one sweep surface where a careless edit is a security
change rather than a styling one: these templates interpolate customer names, addresses, and
product titles into HTML strings that get emailed, and every one of those interpolations is
wrapped in an escaping call sitting inches from the style attributes being rewritten.

This plan also carries the phase's headline claim. Every earlier plan proved its own scope
clean; this one runs the scan across the whole tree once, with nothing left to sweep.

Output: six email builders token-sourced, a before-and-after render comparison, a clean
whole-tree scan, and the phase-close coverage record.
</objective>

<!-- Contracts the executor needs. Do not go looking for these in the codebase. -->

All six builders share one shape: a function composing an HTML string from template literals
with inline `style="..."` attributes, consumed by `sendEmail()` in `lib/email/sender.ts`.
`lib/utils/email.ts` imports `escapeHtmlText` from `@/lib/utils/maintenance-html` and wraps
every customer-supplied value in it. The other builders follow the same convention.

`lib/utils/email.ts` also imports `getStoreConfig` and `postalFooterHtml`/`postalFooterText`
from `@/lib/email/footer`. Add `getThemeTokens` from `@/lib/themes/tokens` alongside those.

Emails use the INVERSE mapping, per D-10 — they stay light under volt-dark. The role-to-field
table is `05-TOKEN-MAP.md` §3b, and it is the same mapping the Stripe form uses.

Snap S10 is the one that lands hardest here. `borderInverse` is `#374151`, chosen because D-05
enumerates it as covering the drawers' border classes; D-10 also routes email dividers to that
same token. The emails' current dividers are much lighter, so they will visibly darken. This is
a consequence of two locked decisions meeting, not a mistake — but it is a real appearance
change that a human should look at rather than a hash comparison approving.

Measured scope: `lib/utils/email.ts` 85 hex occurrences over 536 lines,
`lib/fulfillment/shipping-email.ts` 6 over 353 lines, `lib/subscriptions/lifecycle-email.ts` 2
over 288 lines, `lib/utils/review-notifications.ts` 2, `lib/email/footer.ts` 2,
`lib/payments/refund-email.ts` 1.

Mail clients do not run CSS custom properties, JavaScript, or external stylesheets. Hex must be
interpolated into the string at send time. Do not attempt a `var()` reference in email HTML.
