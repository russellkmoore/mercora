Write the judgement design into the record first, before judging anything, as its own short section.

State the design plainly. Criterion one, renders without error, is judged mechanically across every
captured cell in all 21 runs: each non-missing row must have an image on disk with a non-zero size,
and no row may carry an error note. Criteria two and six — overflow or clipping, and layout matching
its variant's anatomy — vary with the layout combination and are effectively constant across presets
for a given route, so they are judged across all three combinations at one preset. Criteria three,
four and five — legibility, scrim darkness, and the display face — vary with the preset and are
effectively constant across combinations, so task 2 judges them across all seven presets at one
combination. Where the two axes genuinely interact — a light preset under the full-bleed hero, whose
scrim is a literal dark overlay rather than a token — task 2 adds a targeted cross-check.

Say explicitly that this factorisation is a planner assumption, not a proven property, and that a
reader who disagrees should read it as the reason a specific cell was or was not inspected. Record
in the same paragraph that a screen-reader pass over the rendered cells themselves is an open
question this phase inherits rather than closes: the criteria cover visual legibility by inspection
and not an accessibility-tree check, Phase 6 and Phase 6.1 both flagged the same gap and deferred it,
and this phase does the same rather than deciding it silently either way.

Then run the mechanical pass for criterion one across all 21 runs and record its result as a single
findings row, with the real counts.

Then judge criteria two and six. Inspect, for the `volt-dark` label of each combination, the home,
category and product cells at the wide viewport, and the category cell at the narrow viewport for all
three combinations, and the home cell at the narrow viewport for combination C. For each: does the
rendered page match the anatomy the variant contract describes — the right column count, the right
hero shape, the right gallery orientation — and does anything overflow, clip, or push the page
sideways. The narrow-viewport category cells matter most: the list layout's own contract already
carries a flagged overflow risk there.

Record every inspection as a findings row, whether or not anything was wrong. Use the Phase 6.1
column set verbatim and the two judgement literals it established. A row that concludes nothing is
wrong is a finding: it states which capture it was judged from and what was checked.

If an inspection finds a real defect that is fixable inside an existing component with existing
tokens, fix it, commit it on its own with its reason in the message, and record the fixed row citing
the file and line. If it needs a token, a theme-file value or a new variant, do not fix it: record it
as an unresolved finding for a future milestone and say why it is out of scope.

If the category hero appears without a photograph, that is the local fixture having no image URL
configured, not a defect — say so in any row that touches it, rather than judging overlay legibility
from a broken-image capture as though it were real evidence.

light-preset cross-check at combination C.

For each of the seven presets at combination A, inspect the home cell and the product cell at the
wide viewport, and the cart cell in its open state at the wide viewport. Home and product carry
headings, body copy, card hairlines and focus rings against the preset's own surfaces — that is

deliberately reuse the body face for display, and four load a genuinely different face. A heading in
the fallback stack when a distinct face was expected is a regression; a heading in the body face when
that is the declared value is correct. If a face looks absent, check the weight before concluding
anything — three of the four loaded display faces ship no regular cut at all, so a check made at a
default weight false-negatives them. Judge at the weight the heading actually renders.

under combination C, where the full-bleed hero's dark overlay sits over the preset's own light
